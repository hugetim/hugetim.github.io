Decomposing the Epoch Capabilities Index

ECI visualized as a weighted average of benchmarks

Published

November 17, 2026

Updated

November 24, 2026

The Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI) puts 261 AI models on a single scale. What differences in that number ultimately mean boil down to scores on 57 different benchmarks. Figure 1 below shows, for any pair of frontier or near-frontier models, how benchmark scores determine their ECIs.

The math underlying the ECI

Very briefly, an AI model’s score on a given benchmark is modeled as a function of its capability \(C_m\) (also referred to as its ECI): \[ y_{mb} = S\big(\alpha_b \, [C_m - D_b]\big) \tag{1}\]

where \(D_b\) is the benchmark difficulty, \(\alpha_b\) the benchmark slope, and \(S(x) = 1 / (1 + e^{-x})\) is the S-shaped logistic curve rising from 0 to 1. The scatter of the “noise” in each benchmark’s scores is denoted \(\sigma_b\). Epoch fits all the capabilities, difficulties, slopes, and \(\sigma_b\)’s at once as a Bayesian model.

Interpreting a model’s ECI via its scores

Because all parameters are fit jointly, each benchmark score influences the difficulty, slope, and scatter parameters at the same time as it influences a model’s ECI, \(C_m\). As an approximation, though, we can use the estimated values of each benchmark’s difficulty, slope, and \(\sigma_b\) to assess the influence of a given benchmark score on \(C_m\). Each individual benchmark score can then be thought of as a separate measurement of capability: a score \(y_{mb}\) implies \(\tilde{C}_{mb}\), the capability at which that score is most likely.1 A model’s ECI can be thought of as a weighted average of these measurements, \(C_m \approx \sum_b w_{mb} \tilde{C}_{mb} / \sum_b w_{mb}\), with weights that are, approximately,2

\[ w_{mb} \propto \alpha_b^2 \left[\left(\frac{1}{4\sigma_b^2} - 3\right) y_{mb} (1 - y_{mb}) + 1\right] \tag{2}\]

A benchmark score counts most when the corresponding part of that benchmark’s curve is steep and there is little noise. A score near the top or bottom ends of a benchmark counts for less, and one anywhere on a benchmark whose estimated curve is flat counts for little. Otherwise, the scatter \(\sigma_b\) of benchmark noise runs from 0.040 (METR Time Horizons) to 0.179 (MirrorCode), so, other things equal, a mid-curve score on the former counts about 20 times as much as one on the latter.

(The chart below can optionally display analogous measures for an alternative ECI fit, XCI v0.1 . It is the XB+ variant with the capability noise spread allowed to vary by benchmark, \(\tau_b\).)

The effective number of benchmarks behind an ECI can be summarized as \(1 / \sum_b (\text{share}_{mb})^2\): about 7 for Claude 3.5 Sonnet and 10 for Claude Opus 5.5, whose top four benchmarks (WeirdML v3, Remote Labor Index, FrontierSWE, EBR-bench) carry 54% of the weight.

Toward 0% or 100% the two diverge, because the assumed noise there is lopsided: typically by a few ECI points beyond 5% or 95%, and by a dozen or more for scores of exactly 0% or 100% (which Epoch clips to 0.1% and 99.9%).↩︎

  2. Equation 2 approximates the information a score carries about \(C_m\) under Epoch’s likelihood, \([\alpha_b y_{mb} (1 - y_{mb})]^2 \kappa_b^2 [\psi'(y_{mb} \kappa_b) + \psi'((1 - y_{mb}) \kappa_b)]\) with \(\kappa_b = 1 / (4 \sigma_b^2) - 1\) and \(\psi'\) the trigamma function. The weight shares shown in Figure 1 are exact: each is the score’s pull on \(C_m\) divided by its distance \(\tilde{C}_{mb} - C_m\), which makes the weighted average the capability that best fits the model’s scores given every benchmark’s fitted curve and scatter; the expression above is their value for a score whose implied ECI is close to \(C_m\). Not included in the calculations depicted in Figure 1 is the way Epoch’s ECI also averages over uncertainty in the benchmark curves, so the two differ slightly: by 0.04 points for the median model at ECI 150 and above, and at most 0.37. (Epoch does not publish \(\sigma_b\) updates, so the values here come from a refit of Epoch’s published model code, which reproduces its ECI to within sampling error.)↩︎

    A, B = (w[w.model == m].set_index("benchmark") for m in (a, b))
    mid, idx = (E(a) + E(b)) / 2, A.index.union(B.index)
    term = lambda X: (X.share * (X.implied_ECI - mid)).reindex(idx).fillna(0)
